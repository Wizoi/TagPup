## 4. The three most expensive episodes

### E1. Machinery for a handful of cases, built and deleted within the window (cost)

*What it cost.* Built 10-02 to 10-09, deleted 10-10 and 10-11: roots adoption (in the +13,338 merge; ran on the three
libraries, then -3,021 and Verify -1,117), folder markers and relink (+2,757, +680, +1,361; lived two days), the
resumable bulk-edit job (+5,434, six review rounds by the phase 9 retro; lived seven days). Tokens cannot be tied to a
branch (a limit of the tool), but the period that built them cost 77.5 M tokens a merge against 30.1 M after.
*The earliest signal.* The live count was in hand when each was briefed: three libraries, two of them active (DECISIONS
2026-10-02); one library's 8 added folders as the real shape of a gone folder (#925, 10-08); 0 damaged rows. The
phase 9 retro (10-04) counted +30,929 / -1,220 lines in three days and 214 findings, and its actions were about the
review loop, not scope.
*Cheaper path, with what was known then.* A one-off script for three libraries and eight folders, the owner's own rule
of 10-10 ("one-offs are fixed by hand and flagged"); and a scope check at the 10-04 retro, five days before the freeze.
*Who should own the rule.* The brief's live-count line (now in CLAUDE.md, the worker and each charter) and the branch
budget. The budget has no guard: the data file is the only place it is counted, and it counts deletions as size.

### E2. Data risks that sat unranked in the ledger (escape that could have reached the files)

*What it was.* #310 (2026-09-25, no severity): the indexer's identity mint retried any failure with `-m` and put lost
keywords back in a second command outside the journal. It was re-graded `data` as #1355 on 10-15 by the Files health
assessment, and fixing it found #1369 (`data`: a failed read made the forced write carry no keywords). #832 (10-07, no
severity) became #1266 (`data`: a hand-run clustering of 53 minutes overwrote names the owner set meanwhile). No loss is
recorded in the ledger (#1369: no live instance counted); the risk was on the owner's photo files.
*The earliest signal.* The rows themselves: #310's text names lost keywords on its face. The severity rule (10-09) was
applied to new rows only; 664 rows of the window carry none, and 97 rows were open on 10-09.
*Cheaper path.* Grade the open backlog the day the severity rule arrived: one pass over 97 rows. The audit that did it
came on 10-15 and found 44 of 189 open rows (23%) no longer true of the trunk.
*Who should own it.* `tools/add_findings.py`, which accepts a row with no severity word today. New rows already carry
one (430 of 431), so the cheap guard is a refusal there; the general lesson is in section 9.

### E3. 2026-10-15, the health day (cost)

*What it cost.* 22% of the window's tokens in one day: 46 merges, 106 subagent files, 406 commits, 253 findings. Per
merge it cost no more than the rest of the post-freeze period (29.9 M tokens), so the cost is volume, not waste.
*What it bought.* 21 of the window's 48 data/wrong rows, five of which re-grade an earlier row or are marked
pre-existing (#1355, #1356, #1374, #1454, #1468); ruling #1410 out of a branch before merge (a cleanup that could delete the
only copy of a photo, found in round two). All five domains state the seven conditions met.
*The earliest signal of avoidable cost.* Parallel workers in one worktree folder: the worker definition records two
workers' scratch scripts colliding on this day. Cheaper: fewer parallel workers and smaller worker contexts (a sonnet
subagent call averaged 255 k tokens). *Owner of the rule:* the worker's brief (scratch subfolder, done) and a context
budget (action 2).

## 6. Actions (proposed, at most three; the owner decides)

1. **Guard: the branch budget counts added lines and is checked at merge.** A check the main session runs before a
   merge (in `tools/retro_metrics.py` or a small `--branch` mode of it) refuses a branch adding over 1,200 lines unless
   the merge subject says why; a deletion is never over budget. *Metric:* trunk merges adding over 1,200 lines: 33 of
   49 before the freeze, 4 of 85 after. *Target next retro:* at most 2, each with its reason in the subject.
2. **Brief change and hook: a context budget.** `tagpup-worker` gets a `model:` of its own and one line: one concern
   per run; a run whose context passes about 150 k commits what it has and reports, and the rest goes to a fresh
   worker. A UserPromptSubmit hook reads the main transcript's last usage and, above about 300 k, says to hand over and
   `/clear`. *Metric:* calls over 150 k: 55% (64% before the freeze, 45% after); main-session average about 500 k.
   *Target:* under 30%; main session under 200 k; at least one `/clear` per phase.
3. **Delete: the instruction diff of section 7.** *Metric:* CLAUDE.md 322 lines; the five charters and two worker
   definitions 797, with the reviewers 961. *Target:* CLAUDE.md under 200; agent files under 800 with the reviewers.

Not proposed: a guard on severity (new rows already comply, 430 of 431; the backlog is 0) and owner reviews only for
data-path branches (fable, the reviewer and owners, is 2.7% of tokens: not where the cost is).

## 8. The owner's three questions

**(1) "We spent a large amount of Claude quota to get the codebase back into shape: are we on the right path?"**
Yes, with one correction. The health work was the cheaper half: 40% of the window's tokens and 30 M a merge, against
60% and 77.5 M a merge for the week of features before it. It found defects the tests had passed: 21 of the window's 48
data/wrong rows came on 10-15 alone, from the health passes, five of them re-grading an earlier row or marked
pre-existing (E2, E3); and 49 of its 85 merges were net-negative. The correction: it shrank the code by a fifth from its peak (99,330 to 79,612 lines) but the project is
still larger than two weeks ago (code +52%, tests +66%). What is in that growth is mostly what phase 9 built and kept
(library views, the navigator, search, the windowed grid, face boxes, the launcher's hand-over, delete to the Recycle
Bin, derived tables), plus docs (+6,099) and the retro tool (`tools/` +624). The test-to-code ratio rose from 1.07 to
1.17. The freeze removed machinery, not features; whether every kept feature earns its lines was not asked.

**(2) "Is anything further needed?"** Three things, small. The actions of section 6. Two fixes to the retro tool so the
next retro can count what this one could not: tie a subagent run to its branch by the worktree in its `cwd` (meta, not
text), which gives review rounds and runs never merged; and report main sessions over the window and their compactions.
One stale sentence: docs/domains/people.md condition 1 says four contract rows are "still to be written", but #1471 is
closed and the Integration charter holds them. Also parked by the owner's decision: migration 33 on its branch.

**(3) "How do we keep this momentum, here and in other Claude projects?"** By moving each rule out of prose into a
guard as soon as it is broken twice, and by measuring escapes and tokens per merge at every phase end with the tool,
not by adding instructions. Concretely: the health review per change that CLAUDE.md now requires, the budget guard, the
context budget, a net-negative instruction diff at each retro, and the portable set below.

## 9. What to carry to other Claude projects

Portable as is (another step writes the user-level copies):
- `docs/RETRO.md` and `.claude/skills/retro/SKILL.md` (the process, the template, the triggers);
- `tools/retro_metrics.py`, its GIT, CLAUDE CODE SESSION TRANSCRIPTS and WORKERS sections (with the two fixes above);
- `tools/patch.py` and `.claude/hooks/no-heredoc-escapes.mjs` (exact edits; no mangled escapes);
- `tools/add_findings.py`, `tools/stale_findings.py` and the ledger's row format with severity words (`data`, `wrong`,
  `low`) and provisional numbers per branch;
- the agent roster's shape: worker, small worker, reviewer, quick reviewer, each with an explicit `model:` line;
- the meta-rules: a number told to the owner is counted this session; a one-way choice is asked first; the second fix
  for one symptom is an ownership question; a special case needs a real instance; a write reports what it changed;
  a fix's test fails on the old code; test data is made by the code that makes the real thing.

Adapt: the single-owner guard pattern (`tests/test_rules_have_one_owner.py`: a regex per forbidden spelling and its
owner) and the test map (`tests/test_the_test_map.py`, every test file one owner) to the new project's rules; the
branch budget and the context budget; domain owners and the seven conditions (docs/OWNERS.md) only once a project has
domains worth owning. Learned here and general: **a new rule is applied to the backlog the day it is made** (E2); a
size budget counts what is added, never what is deleted (section 2); a model setting left to inheritance runs the
longest contexts on whatever the main session happens to be.

Do not carry: every TagPup rule (paths, ExifTool, the GPU, settings, faces), the five charters, the scenario ids,
INVARIANTS.md.

*Limits of this draft.* Review rounds per branch, runs never merged, restarts and the time a person spent are not in
the record; who found a row is a text proxy; which agent ran on which model on 10-15 (fable: 0 turns that day) is
unknown; the prices are placeholders and fable is unpriced, so a dollar figure is a scale, not a bill. See the open questions file.
